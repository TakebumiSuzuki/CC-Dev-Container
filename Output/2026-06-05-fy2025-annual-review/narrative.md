# Meridian Consulting Group — FY2025 Annual Performance Review

Author: Executive Leadership Team
Date: 2026-06-05
Audience: Executive Leadership Team
Duration: 30 minutes

## Executive Summary

FY2025 delivered MCG's third consecutive year of double-digit revenue growth. Revenue reached $43.7M (+13% YoY), operating margin held at 35.8%, and all five practices grew in line with firm targets. Client retention remained strong at 89%, and hiring nearly hit plan. The binding challenge heading into FY2026 is execution quality: a net NPS of –19 in FY2025 client surveys and 55% project on-time delivery signal that capacity and project management discipline must improve alongside continued commercial growth.

## Financial Performance

### Revenue Growth

MCG grew revenue from $38.7M in FY2024 to $43.7M in FY2025 — the third consecutive year of 13%+ growth. Q4 was the strongest quarter at $11.7M. Growth was broad-based: all five practices expanded between 12% and 14% YoY.

| Category   | Count | Share |

### Talent and Attrition

MCG ended FY2025 at 105 consultants. Nine departures were logged — 7 voluntary, 2 involuntary — for an 8.6% total attrition rate. Voluntary attrition benchmark for the industry is 16.5%; MCG's rate of 14.8% (per benchmarks) is below average but above top-quartile (11.0%). Of known destinations, two left for McKinsey & Company and one for Bain & Company — a signal of talent quality but also a retention risk at senior grades.

Attrition was concentrated in Technology & Digital (4 departures) and Finance & Risk (4 departures), the two highest-growth practices.

(Source: ./Data/people/attrition_log.csv)

## Conclusion

FY2025 confirmed MCG's commercial engine is functioning: consistent 13% revenue growth, stable margins, near-complete hiring, and strong retention in the firm's core industries. The story heading into FY2026 is less about growing the top line and more about earning the right to grow it faster — by closing the gap on project delivery reliability and client satisfaction. A net NPS of –19 and 55% on-time delivery are solvable with targeted investment in project management discipline and senior-grade continuity on accounts.

### Key Takeaways

- Revenue $43.7M (+13% YoY); operating margin 35.8%; all five practices growing in line
- 89% client retention; net NPS of –19 signals execution quality gaps, particularly in communication and project management
- 73.5% avg utilization; 9 FY2025 departures (8.6% rate); hiring on plan at 55/56
- $48M weighted pipeline for FY2026; top loss driver is relationship gaps, not price or quality
- FY2026 priority: close delivery reliability and NPS gap to unlock top-quartile growth trajectory

### Anticipated Q&A

- *Why is NPS negative if client retention is 89%?* Retention reflects contractual decisions, often made by procurement or budget holders; NPS captures the working experience of day-to-day contacts. The 89% retention and –19 NPS can coexist when economic switching costs are high but satisfaction is not yet a strength.
- *What's driving the 55% on-time delivery rate?* The project profitability and attrition data both point to staffing continuity — particularly mid-engagement reassignments in Technology & Digital and Finance & Risk. Addressing senior attrition in those practices should improve delivery consistency.
- *Is the Q4 utilization dip structural or seasonal?* Likely seasonal: Q4 typically sees slower deal conversion and year-end client budget freezes. The FY2026 pipeline is well-loaded at Verbal Commit and Negotiation stages, suggesting Q1 ramp should be solid.
- *The win rate of 29% — is that a pipeline quality issue or a BD process issue?* The primary loss reason is relationship gap, not product or price — which points to BD process and executive account coverage rather than our service quality. The FY2026 pipeline is weighted toward existing-client upsell (Operations Excellence and Strategy & Growth) where relationship strength is higher.
- *Are we at risk of losing more talent to McKinsey and Bain?* Two departures to top-tier firms in a year is not alarming at this headcount, but the concentration in Technology & Digital — our largest and fastest-growing practice — warrants a targeted retention review for senior consultants and managers in that practice.

# FY2025 Annual Performance Review — Meridian Consulting Group

Author: Office of the COO
Date: 2026-06-08
Audience: Executive Leadership Team
Duration: 30 minutes

## Executive Summary

FY2025 was a year of disciplined, profitable growth. Firm revenue reached **$43.7M, up 13% year-over-year**, the third consecutive year of double-digit growth, while operating margin held steady at **35.8%** — proof that we scaled without diluting profitability. Client retention recovered to **89%** and engagement scores rose to **72/100**, both moving in the right direction after softer FY2024 readings.

The year was not without friction, and this review presents it plainly. On-time project delivery fell to **55%**, our competitive win rate is running below the industry benchmark, and the client NPS mix skewed toward detractors. Underneath several of these signals is the same root cause we flagged in the engagement survey: capacity and development pressure on early-tenure and Technology & Digital staff. This deck walks the leadership team through performance across five pillars — Financials, Clients, People, Delivery, and Market Position — and frames the investments that protect FY2026 growth.

## Financial Performance

### Revenue and Profitability Trend

FY2025 extended a steady multi-year growth trajectory. Revenue grew from $38.7M to **$43.7M (+13%)**, and operating profit rose in lockstep to **$15.7M**. The most important point for this audience is the flat margin line: across three years of ~15–17% annual revenue growth, operating margin has not moved off ~35.7%, indicating our cost-to-serve is scaling proportionally rather than eroding.

| Fiscal Year | Revenue ($M) | Operating Profit ($M) | Operating Margin |
| ----------- | -----------: | --------------------: | ---------------: |
| FY2023      |         33.4 |                  11.9 |            35.7% |
| FY2024      |         38.7 |                  13.8 |            35.6% |
| FY2025      |         43.7 |                  15.7 |            35.8% |

(Source: financials/monthly_financials.csv)

Margin stability at this growth rate is the headline financial story — it is what makes the FY2026 reinvestment case affordable rather than dilutive.

### Revenue by Practice

Growth was broad-based: every practice grew 12–14% YoY, with no single practice carrying the firm. Technology & Digital remains the largest at **$12.3M**, followed by Strategy & Growth at $9.6M. The even spread is a resilience signal — we are not over-indexed on one offering — though it is worth noting that Technology & Digital, our growth engine, is also the practice flagging the most acute capacity strain (see People).

| Practice               | FY2025 Revenue ($M) | YoY Growth |
| ---------------------- | ------------------: | ---------: |
| Technology & Digital   |                12.3 |     +13.1% |
| Strategy & Growth      |                 9.6 |     +12.0% |
| Operations Excellence  |                 8.7 |     +13.5% |
| Finance & Risk         |                 7.8 |     +13.3% |
| HR & People            |                 5.2 |     +13.0% |

(Source: financials/quarterly_revenue.csv)

The balance across practices is a deliberate strength, but it places a premium on staffing the Technology & Digital pipeline without burning out the team delivering it.

## Client Health

### Retention

Client retention rebounded to **89%** in FY2025, recovering most of the ground lost in FY2024 (85%) though still below the FY2023 high of 93%. The FY2025 non-renewals were spread across pricing, competitive switching, and budget cuts rather than concentrated in any single failure mode — no systemic churn driver.

| Fiscal Year | Renewal Rate |
| ----------- | -----------: |
| FY2023      |          93% |
| FY2024      |          85% |
| FY2025      |          89% |

(Source: clients/client_retention.csv)

The recovery is encouraging, but two of the FY2025 losses were to direct competitors — a thread we pick up in Market Position.

### Net Promoter Mix

Average NPS sits around the industry midpoint, but the underlying FY2025 distribution deserves an honest read: detractors (50) outnumbered promoters (29) across 112 surveys. This is the clearest "watch" signal on the client side and aligns with the delivery friction below — clients notice when timelines slip.

| Sentiment | FY2025 Responses |
| --------- | ---------------: |
| Promoter  |               29 |
| Passive   |               33 |
| Detractor |               50 |

(Source: clients/nps_survey_results.csv)

We should not let a midpoint *average* mask a detractor-heavy *mix*; closing the delivery gap is the most direct lever on this score.

## People and Talent

### Engagement Scores

Overall engagement rose to **72/100** (from 69), with gains in leadership satisfaction and willingness to recommend MCG as an employer. Two categories moved the other way — career-growth clarity and work-life balance both declined — and leadership has already committed follow-up actions (practice-specific promotion rubrics, a utilization cap, semi-annual comp reviews).

| Metric                          | FY2024 | FY2025 |
| ------------------------------- | -----: | -----: |
| Overall engagement              |     69 |     72 |
| Would recommend MCG as employer |    63% |    68% |
| Plan to stay 2+ years           |    57% |    61% |
| Satisfaction with leadership    |     71 |     74 |
| Satisfaction with career growth |     65 |     61 |
| Work-life balance               |     60 |     58 |

(Source: people/team_survey_fy25.txt)

The declines are concentrated, not broad: career-growth clarity at the Consultant→Senior and Manager→Senior Manager transitions, and utilization pressure in Technology & Digital, which scored work-life balance at just 48/100 (Source: people/team_survey_fy25.txt).

### Workforce and Utilization

The firm closed FY2025 with **105 consultants** and near-perfect execution against the hiring plan (55 actual vs. 56 planned). Average utilization was **73.5%**, healthy headroom below the burnout line in aggregate — but the aggregate hides the Technology & Digital strain noted above. Voluntary attrition was contained at roughly 9 departures, with early-tenure Analysts and Consultants the highest flight-risk cohort.

| Practice               | FY2025 Avg Utilization |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------: |
| Strategy & Growth      |                  75.6% |
| Technology & Digital   |                  74.0% |
| Operations Excellence  |                  73.6% |
| HR & People            |                  73.0% |
| Finance & Risk         |                  71.7% |

(Source: people/monthly_utilization.csv)

Utilization is balanced at the firm level; the talent risk is specific and addressable — early-tenure development clarity and targeted Tech & Digital hiring, both already owned actions.

## Delivery Excellence

FY2025 closed **31 projects** at a healthy average margin of **30.6%** and a solid client satisfaction score of **4.15/5**. The standout concern is **on-time delivery at 55%** — nearly half of completed engagements ran past their planned end date. This is the operational counterpart to the detractor-heavy NPS mix, and the most actionable delivery problem we carry into FY2026 [needs-verification].

| Delivery Metric          | FY2025 |

(Source: projects/completed_projects.csv)

Margin per practice held up well — Finance & Risk (32.6%) and Technology & Digital (31.6%) led — so the issue is schedule discipline and staffing timing, not unprofitable work.

Profitability of delivery is sound; the lever to pull is predictability of delivery, which feeds directly back into client sentiment.

## Pipeline and Market Position

### Competitive Win/Loss

FY2025 competitive decisions were a clear area to strengthen: of 24 resolved competitive opportunities, we won 7 and lost 17 — a **29% win rate by count**, with **$6.5M won against $13.8M lost**. Losses clustered on relationship gaps and timing rather than capability, and recurred against MBB and Big Four names entering above the formal process.

| Outcome | Deals | Value ($M) |
| ------- | ----: | ---------: |
| Won     |     7 |        6.5 |
| Lost    |    17 |       13.8 |

(Source: pipeline/win_loss_analysis.csv)

The qualitative debriefs are consistent: our model of keeping senior consultants in the work is a genuine differentiator in the $500K–$1.5M range, while our losses trace to C-suite relationship gaps we can invest against (Source: pipeline/competitive_intel.txt). The open pipeline is substantial — roughly **$48M weighted** across 80 opportunities — so the constraint is conversion, not top-of-funnel volume (Source: pipeline/sales_pipeline.csv).

### How We Compare to the Market

Benchmarked against the industry, MCG sits comfortably above average on most operating metrics and approaches top-quartile on margin and revenue per consultant — while trailing the best on win rate and client NPS, the two areas this review has flagged repeatedly.

| Metric                          | MCG  | Industry Avg | Top Quartile |
| ------------------------------- | ---: | -----------: | -----------: |
| Consultant utilization (%)      | 76.2 |         73.5 |         80.1 |
| Revenue per consultant ($K)     |  342 |          318 |          390 |
| Average project margin (%)      | 31.4 |         28.0 |         36.0 |
| Win rate on competitive RFPs (%)|   39 |           33 |           46 |
| Client NPS (0–100)              |   52 |           44 |           65 |
| Revenue growth YoY (%)          | 12.8 |          8.5 |         18.0 |
| Voluntary attrition (%)         | 14.8 |         16.5 |         11.0 |
| Revenue from repeat clients (%) |   68 |           60 |           75 |

(Source: market/industry_benchmarks.csv)

The pattern is consistent with everything above: MCG is a healthy, above-market performer whose next tier of growth is gated by conversion and delivery consistency, not by demand or profitability.

## Conclusion

FY2025 was a strong, profitable year that also gave leadership a clear and honest punch-list. We grew 13% at a stable 36% margin, retained more clients, and lifted engagement — and we did so while carrying real friction in delivery predictability, competitive conversion, and early-tenure talent development. The encouraging part is that these issues share roots and already have owners and committed actions. The decision in front of this team is to fund those actions at the pace FY2026 growth requires.

### Key Takeaways

- **Profitable growth held**: revenue $43.7M (+13% YoY) at a stable 35.8% operating margin.
- **Client signals mixed but improving**: retention recovered to 89%; NPS mix remains detractor-heavy.
- **Engagement up, with two flags**: overall 72/100, but career-growth clarity and work-life balance declined, concentrated in early-tenure and Technology & Digital staff.
- **Delivery is profitable but not predictable**: 30.6% margin and 4.15 CSAT, undermined by 55% on-time delivery.
- **Demand isn't the constraint, conversion is**: ~$48M weighted pipeline against a 29% competitive win rate — the clearest FY2026 growth lever.

### Anticipated Q&A

- *Revenue grew 13% — why reinvest now instead of banking the margin?* Because the margin is exactly what makes reinvestment affordable: at a stable 36% margin, funding promotion rubrics, targeted Tech & Digital hiring, and delivery-PM capacity protects the growth rate without diluting profitability.
- *Is the 55% on-time delivery rate a quality problem or a staffing problem?* Primarily staffing and schedule discipline, not quality — margins (30.6%) and CSAT (4.15) held up, so the work is good and profitable; it is finishing on schedule that slipped, which is what the delivery-capacity investment targets.
- *Our win rate trails the benchmark — is that a capability gap?* No — debriefs point to C-suite relationship and timing gaps, not capability; we lose late, above the formal process, to MBB/Big Four brand relationships, which is why the FY2026 plan invests in partner-led account relationships rather than new service lines.

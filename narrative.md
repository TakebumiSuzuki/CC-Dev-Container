## Executive Summary

FY2025 was another year of strong growth for Meridian Consulting Group. Firm-wide revenue reached $43.7M, a 13.0% increase over FY2024's $38.7M and the third consecutive year of double-digit top-line growth. Operating profit grew in step — $15.7M at a 35.8% margin — demonstrating that the firm is scaling without margin erosion. All five practices contributed to growth in a narrow 12–13.5% band, reflecting broad-based momentum rather than reliance on a single engine.

The headline, however, sits alongside a delivery quality challenge that requires direct attention. On-time project completion fell to 54.8% in FY2025, down from 68.8% the prior year. Client satisfaction averaged 4.15/5.0 — marginally better than FY2024 — but six completed engagements scored below 3.5, and NPS remained essentially flat. Client retention recovered from its FY2024 trough (85.3%) to 89.3%, a positive signal, but the firm has not yet returned to FY2023's 93.3% high.

Looking forward, the active project portfolio stands at $48.5M in contracted work across 58 engagements, with 85% of projects on track or ahead of schedule. The immediate priority for FY2026 is converting strong revenue momentum into stronger delivery consistency.

## Financial Performance

### Revenue Growth

Firm revenue has grown every year since FY2023, reaching $43.7M in FY2025. The compound annual growth rate over the three-year window is approximately 14.4%. Critically, operating margin has held in a 35.6–35.8% band throughout — top-line growth has not come at the expense of profitability.

| Fiscal Year | Revenue | Operating Profit | Margin |
| ----------- | ------: | ---------------: | -----: |
| FY2023      | $33.4M  | $11.9M           | 35.7%  |

(Source: ./Data/financials/monthly_financials.csv)

The consistency of margin across a period when the firm added significant revenue is a meaningful signal: the cost structure is scaling efficiently and there is no evidence of unsustainable discounting or cost overruns at the firm level.

## Project Delivery Quality

### On-Time Completion and Client Satisfaction

FY2025 saw 31 completed projects with a combined contract value of $20.5M. The on-time completion rate of 54.8% is a material step back from FY2024's 68.8% — the sharpest decline in recent years and the most significant operational concern surfaced by this review. At the same time, average client satisfaction of 4.15/5.0 improved slightly from FY2024's 4.10, suggesting that when projects do deliver, the quality of work is strong.

| Practice              | Projects | On-Time Rate | Avg. Satisfaction | Avg. Margin |
| --------------------- | -------: | -----------: | ----------------: | ----------: |
| Operations Excellence |        6 | 100%         | 3.92              | 29.8%       |
| Strategy & Growth     |        4 | 50%          | 4.50              | 27.1%       |
| Technology & Digital  |        7 | 43%          | 4.09              | 31.6%       |
| HR & People           |        7 | 43%          | 4.44              | 30.3%       |
| Finance & Risk        |        7 | 43%          | 3.91              | 32.6%       |

(Source: ./Data/projects/completed_projects.csv)

Operations Excellence was the only practice to achieve 100% on-time delivery, though its average satisfaction score (3.92) was the second lowest, suggesting that on-time delivery alone does not drive client perception. Strategy & Growth and HR & People posted the highest satisfaction scores (4.50 and 4.44 respectively), even with below-average on-time rates — pointing to a practice culture that manages client expectations effectively when timelines slip.

Six of the 31 completed projects scored below 3.5 on client satisfaction. These are disproportionate drivers of NPS risk and merit individual account reviews.

FY2025 included several standout engagements that underscore the firm's capabilities at their best:

- **Apex Financial Corp — Finance Function Transformation (P2241):** $1.1M engagement, delivered two weeks ahead of schedule. The client CFO provided a reference call to a prospective client. Margin came in above the Finance & Risk practice average at 34%. A follow-on regulatory compliance opportunity is in the negotiation stage. (Source: ./Data/projects/project_notes.txt)
- **Cardinal Health Systems — Workforce Planning (P2267):** Client satisfaction score of 4.8, led by Senior Manager Angela Torres across three hospital networks. The engagement produced a repeatable workforce planning methodology now being packaged as a proprietary MCG framework. Two additional health system prospects have been referred directly from this client. (Source: ./Data/projects/project_notes.txt)
- **Quantum Computing Inc — Technology Architecture Review (P2289):** Technology & Digital practice, four consultants. Final score 4.6/5.0. The client's CTO described the output as "the clearest technical roadmap we've ever received from an external adviser." (Source: ./Data/projects/project_notes.txt)

### Delivery Issues

Two engagements in FY2025 surfaced systemic lessons:

- **Triumph Automotive — M&A Integration Strategy (P2278):** Ran six weeks over schedule due to mid-engagement scope expansion. A contract amendment was processed but late, creating a Q3 cash flow gap. Client satisfaction at project close was 3.7; it recovered to 5.4 following a post-project executive debrief. The relationship is intact but renewal risk on the follow-on implementation has been flagged. Root cause: scope gates were not built into the SOW explicitly. (Source: ./Data/projects/project_notes.txt)
- **Empire Retail Group — Supply Chain Optimization (P2303):** Partial delivery engagement that closed at $320K of a $500K contract following three executive sponsor changes during the engagement. Final satisfaction score not collected. Decision was made not to pursue further work with this client given organizational instability. (Source: ./Data/projects/project_notes.txt)

Both cases point to the same underlying risk: engagements are vulnerable when scope or sponsorship changes mid-stream and the firm's SOW and escalation protocols do not keep pace.

## Client Health

### Retention

Client retention rebounded to 89.3% in FY2025, recovering from the FY2024 dip to 85.3%. Eight clients churned in FY2025, with pricing and competitive loss accounting for four of those departures. The retention recovery is encouraging, but the firm has not returned to the FY2023 level of 93.3%.

| Fiscal Year | Total Clients | Renewed | Churned | Retention Rate |
| ----------- | ------------: | ------: | ------: | -------------: |
| FY2023      | 75            | 70      | 5       | 93.3%          |
| FY2024      | 75            | 64      | 11      | 85.3%          |
| FY2025      | 75            | 67      | 8       | 89.3%          |

(Source: ./Data/clients/client_retention.csv)

Of the eight FY2025 churns, two were lost to pricing (suggesting competitive pricing pressure or perceived value gap), two were won by competitors, two were budget-driven, one was a relationship change, and one was a natural scope completion. The pricing and competitive losses warrant the most attention, as they signal active re-evaluation by clients rather than passive churn.

### NPS

NPS results for FY2025 were essentially flat versus FY2024. The average score of 54.5 is virtually unchanged from 54.3. The promoter share declined slightly from 29% to 26%, while passives increased from 27% to 29% and detractors held constant at 45%.

| Category   | FY2024 (n=112) | FY2025 (n=112) |
| ---------- | -------------: | -------------: |
| Promoters (≥70) | 32 (29%)  | 29 (26%)       |
| Passives (50–69) | 30 (27%) | 33 (29%)       |
| Detractors (<50) | 50 (45%) | 50 (45%)       |
| Avg. NPS score | 54.3        | 54.5           |

(Source: ./Data/clients/nps_survey_results.csv)

A flat NPS in a year of strong revenue growth is, at best, a neutral signal — the firm is winning more business without materially improving how existing clients perceive the relationship. The NPS follow-up call themes are instructive: analytical rigor and responsiveness are the firm's strongest perceived attributes (cited by 18 and 14 of 28 respondents respectively), while consultant continuity mid-engagement and the translation of recommendations into operational action plans are the top improvement areas (cited by 9 and 8 respondents) (Source: ./Data/clients/key_client_feedback.txt).

The detractor share at 45% is the number that warrants a dedicated remediation track — not because the average score is alarming, but because that many clients at risk of churn represents a meaningful revenue and referral exposure at the firm's current scale.

## Strategic Priorities for FY2026

Based on the FY2025 data and field observations, three priorities stand out for the leadership team's attention:

**1. Restore project delivery consistency.** The drop in on-time delivery from 68.8% to 54.8% is the most operationally urgent finding in this review. The Triumph Automotive and Empire Retail cases both point to inadequate scope and sponsorship change protocols. A firm-wide SOW standard for transformation engagements — with explicit scope gates and executive sponsor change escalation clauses — would address the root cause directly rather than treating individual project failures as isolated incidents.

**2. Convert detractors and improve NPS structural mix.** With 45% of surveyed clients in the detractor band, the firm is running a meaningful retention risk that is not visible in the retention rate alone (since detractors may not churn immediately). A targeted account recovery program for clients scoring below 50 — combined with structural improvements in consultant continuity and the translation of recommendations into action plans — addresses the top two improvement themes that clients named.

**3. Resolve the Technology & Digital capacity constraint.** The combination of the highest external pipeline ($6.2M), highest revenue share (28% of firm), and over-capacity utilization creates an imminent revenue ceiling. Approving the two Senior Consultant hires requested by the practice lead is the minimum action required to avoid lost pipeline conversion in FY2026's strongest-demand practice.

## Conclusion

FY2025 demonstrated that Meridian Consulting Group can sustain double-digit revenue growth with stable margins across all five practices simultaneously. The $43.7M result and $15.7M operating profit represent the firm's strongest financial year on record. The active backlog of $48.5M positions FY2026 for continued growth from a position of strength.

The work ahead is to make delivery quality match financial performance. On-time rates, NPS composition, and the detractor share all indicate that the firm's operational infrastructure has not fully kept pace with its growth. Addressing this gap in FY2026 — through stronger SOW standards, consultant continuity commitments, and targeted capacity investment in Technology & Digital — will determine whether FY2025's growth translates into durable client relationships or remains primarily volume-driven.

### Key Takeaways

- FY2025 revenue reached $43.7M (+13.0% YoY), the third consecutive year of double-digit growth; operating margin held at 35.8%.
- All five practices grew 12–13.5% — growth is broad-based, not dependent on a single practice.
- On-time project completion declined to 54.8% (from 68.8% in FY2024); this is the primary operational risk heading into FY2026.
- Client retention recovered to 89.3%, up from FY2024's 85.3% trough; NPS is flat with 45% of clients still in the detractor band.
- Active backlog of $48.5M across 58 projects provides strong FY2026 revenue visibility; Technology & Digital capacity must be resolved to realize the pipeline.

### Anticipated Q&A

- *Revenue grew 13% but on-time delivery fell to 55% — are these connected?* Partially. Rapid growth can stretch delivery teams, but the specific root causes identified — inadequate scope gates (Triumph Automotive) and volatile client-side sponsorship (Empire Retail) — are process failures rather than pure capacity failures. The fix is structural, not just hiring.
- *Why is NPS flat despite strong retention and revenue growth?* Retention measures whether clients renew contracts; NPS measures how they feel about the relationship. A client can renew out of switching costs or inertia without becoming a promoter. The divergence signals that revenue metrics are healthy but relationship depth is not deepening at the same rate.
- *What is the risk on the six At Risk active projects?* The six at-risk projects represent approximately 10% of the active portfolio by count. Individual account reviews are recommended at the next QBR to assess remediation options before any of these move toward partial delivery or early termination.
- *Is the Technology & Digital capacity constraint impacting FY2025 results, or is it purely a forward risk?* The over-capacity situation was flagged as of April 2026 (Q1 2026 observation), so it did not depress FY2025 results. It is a forward constraint: the $6.2M weighted sales pipeline in that practice cannot convert to revenue at full efficiency without additional headcount.
- *What was the total revenue impact of the two delivery issue engagements (Triumph Automotive, Empire Retail)?* Empire Retail invoiced $320K of a $500K contract — a $180K shortfall. Triumph Automotive's scope amendment was eventually processed, so the primary impact was a cash flow timing gap in Q3 rather than lost revenue. Combined revenue leakage is estimated at approximately $180K, against a firm total of $43.7M — immaterial to the top line but meaningful as a leading indicator of delivery risk.

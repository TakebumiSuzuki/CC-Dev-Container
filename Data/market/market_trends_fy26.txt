MARKET TRENDS & OUTLOOK — FY2026
Meridian Consulting Group | Strategy & Business Development
Prepared: January 2026

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MACROECONOMIC CONTEXT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The consulting market entered 2026 in a cautiously optimistic posture following
a period of cost-driven budget freezes in H2 2024. Corporate capex is recovering,
particularly in financial services and healthcare, where regulatory mandates are
creating non-discretionary consulting demand. The technology consulting segment
benefited from AI-related transformation projects beginning to move from pilot
to full deployment budgets, contributing to an estimated 9% sector growth rate
in FY2025 (Source: Kennedy Research, Q4 2025 estimate).

Inflationary pressure on labor costs has stabilized. MCG's FY2026 compensation
budget was set at +4.5% base salary across all grades, broadly in line with
industry benchmarks. Rate card increases of 3–5% were successfully passed
through on approximately 70% of retainer contract renewals in Q4 2025.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEMAND TRENDS BY SECTOR
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Financial Services:
High demand for regulatory compliance and risk management advisory, driven by
new capital adequacy requirements effective January 2026 and ongoing Basel IV
implementation in the banking sector. Asset management clients are seeking
efficiency studies as fee compression accelerates. M&A advisory activity is
picking up after a subdued 2024.

Healthcare:
Post-pandemic operational normalization is largely complete. The new wave of
demand is centered on workforce planning (nursing shortage is systemic), revenue
cycle optimization, and technology integration across merged health systems.
Private equity-backed healthcare rollups are a growing source of M&A integration
mandates.

Manufacturing:
Supply chain resilience projects continue to generate steady work. ESG reporting
requirements (SEC climate disclosure rules phased in from 2026) are creating
new demand for sustainability strategy and reporting framework design. Automotive
sector is slower; EV transition uncertainty is delaying some transformation programs.

Energy:
Regulatory-driven demand is strongest here. Utility companies are seeking help
with rate case preparation and grid modernization strategy. The energy transition
is generating scenario planning and portfolio restructuring mandates. MCG has
limited existing relationships in this sector — targeted outreach is underway.

Technology & Professional Services:
AI strategy mandates are the single fastest-growing request category in our
pipeline. Clients want help defining AI governance, identifying automation
opportunities, and building business cases for generative AI deployment. This
is a meaningful opportunity for MCG's Technology & Digital and Strategy practices
to co-deliver.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TALENT MARKET OBSERVATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The consulting talent market tightened in H2 2025 relative to the prior two years.
Post-tech-layoff absorption is largely complete; former tech-sector talent that
entered consulting during 2023–2024 has reduced the supply-side pressure at the
Analyst and Consultant levels. The competition for experienced Senior Consultants
and Managers (3–8 years) remains acute — these candidates are receiving offers
from in-house strategy functions, particularly at tech companies and private equity
portfolio companies.

MBA recruiting: MCG is not a target school recruiter at the top-10 MBA programs.
Our hires come primarily from experienced lateral hiring and from second-tier MBA
programs where we have stronger relationships. This is a medium-term talent risk
if we intend to grow the Strategy & Growth practice's share of high-complexity
strategy mandates.

Remote/hybrid norms: Clients in financial services and healthcare are increasingly
expecting on-site presence (3–4 days per week). This is pushing back against the
remote-first norm that emerged during 2022–2023. MCG's policy of client-determined
work location is the right call for retention, but requires proactive conversation
during the proposal phase about realistic on-site expectations.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR MCG FY2026
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. Double down on Technology & Digital. The AI strategy wave is real and MCG
   has credibility with existing clients to expand into AI advisory. The practice
   needs headcount before it can capture the demand in the pipeline.

2. Build a Healthcare specialty. The workforce planning and revenue cycle wins
   are a foundation. Two more senior hires with healthcare-specific credentials
   would justify a dedicated sub-practice identity.

3. Defend financial services accounts from Big Four encroachment. The regulatory
   compliance wave means clients will have more Big Four conversations. MCG needs
   to be proactive about positioning as the complement (agility, senior attention)
   rather than the alternative.

4. Energy sector entry. The ESG and grid modernization mandates represent a
   greenfield opportunity where MCG has no legacy baggage. Consider a targeted
   lateral hire from a utility or regulatory advisory background as a beachhead.

5. Invest in AI advisory capability. At minimum, a structured offering and trained
   team of 4–5 consultants could capture $3–4M in addressable pipeline that is
   currently going to competitors or being deferred by clients with no advisor.

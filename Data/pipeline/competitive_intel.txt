─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECENT COMPETITIVE LOSSES — ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Lost to Deloitte — Northern Energy Systems (RFP, March 2026, est. $900K)
Deloitte won on brand recognition and claimed a "pre-built regulatory compliance
accelerator" for energy sector clients. Our proposal was technically stronger, per
the client's own debrief, but the enterprise risk function at NES has a standing
preference for Big Four on compliance-adjacent work. Recommendation: Build a
one-page "MCG vs. Big Four" positioning sheet specifically for risk and compliance
RFPs in regulated sectors.

Lost to McKinsey — Omega Asset Management (sole-source approach, Jan 2026, est. $1.2M)
McKinsey engaged the CEO of Omega directly through a board relationship before the
formal scoping process began. By the time our account manager was aware of the
opportunity, the decision was effectively made. This is the third time in 18 months
that a McKinsey "above-the-sponsor" entry has cost us a financial services win.
Recommendation: Invest in C-suite relationship development at our top 15 target
accounts. Partners need to be present at industry events, not just at project kickoffs.

Lost to PwC — Heritage Bank (competitive RFP, Feb 2026, est. $650K)
PwC's proposal included a fintech ecosystem partnership component that we could not
match. Heritage Bank is going through a digital core banking transformation and wanted
an advisor with embedded fintech relationships. Gap identified: MCG lacks formal
technology alliance partnerships. Exploring partnership conversations with two fintech
advisory boutiques.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECENT COMPETITIVE WINS — WHAT WORKED
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Won vs. BCG — Redwood Capital (Q4 2025, $720K)
BCG was the incumbent. We won by demonstrating a 30% faster mobilization timeline
and providing two client references in private equity with near-identical mandates.
The decision maker cited "BCG's team felt too senior and too removed from the work."
This is a recurring theme — our model of keeping senior consultants actively in the
work (rather than delegating to junior staff) is a genuine differentiator in the
$500K–$1.5M deal range.

Won vs. Accenture — Riverside Manufacturing (Q1 2026, $1.1M)
Accenture submitted a technology-heavy proposal centered on an ERP platform
implementation. We positioned as "technology-agnostic strategy first," which
resonated with the CFO who had a prior bad experience with a vendor-driven
transformation. Lesson: In manufacturing, lead with operational outcomes, not
technology platforms.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MARKET POSITIONING OBSERVATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The mid-market consulting segment ($200K–$2M engagements) is being squeezed from
two directions:
1. Boutique specialists (domain-specific, low overhead) are undercutting on price
   in niche verticals like healthcare IT and ESG advisory.
2. Large firms (Deloitte, Accenture) are increasingly willing to bid on smaller
   engagements as a land-and-expand strategy, accepting lower initial margins.

MCG's defensible position remains in multi-practice engagements where we can
assemble cross-functional teams quickly. Single-practice RFPs under $300K are
where we face the most price pressure and should be selective.

Pricing intelligence (from client debriefs and sourced conversations, Q1 2026):
- McKinsey blended rate for strategy work: ~$650–$750/hr
- Deloitte blended rate for implementation: ~$280–$380/hr
- BCG blended rate for strategy: ~$580–$680/hr
- MCG blended rate (current book of business): ~$295/hr
- Boutique competitors (e.g., West Monroe, Huron): ~$220–$280/hr

The rate data suggests MCG is priced at a justified premium over boutiques but
remains competitive below the MBB tier. We should not compete on price against
boutiques — compete on breadth of expertise and track record.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
NOTABLE WINS — FY2025
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

APEX FINANCIAL CORP — Finance Function Transformation (P2241)
One of the largest engagements of the year at $1.1M. The client's CFO personally
acknowledged the quality of our work in a reference call with a prospective client
(Sapphire Financial). Delivered two weeks ahead of schedule. Margin came in at 34%,
above the Finance & Risk practice average. The relationship opened a second opportunity
in regulatory compliance currently in the Negotiation stage.

CARDINAL HEALTH SYSTEMS — Workforce Planning (P2267)
A genuinely complex engagement involving three hospital networks and a union negotiation
backdrop. Team of 8, led by Senior Manager Angela Torres. Client satisfaction score: 4.8.
The engagement surfaced a repeatable workforce planning methodology that is now being
packaged as an MCG proprietary framework. Two additional health system prospects have
been referred directly from this client in Q1 2026.

QUANTUM COMPUTING INC — Technology Architecture Review (P2289)
Fast-moving client in a sector where we had limited prior experience. The team
(Technology & Digital practice, 4 consultants) had to ramp quickly on distributed
systems architecture. Final score: 4.6/5.0. The engagement produced a 47-page
architecture blueprint that the client's CTO described as "the clearest technical
roadmap we've ever received from an external adviser."

PROJECTS WITH DELIVERY ISSUES — FY2025
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

TRIUMPH AUTOMOTIVE — M&A Integration Strategy (P2278)
Ran 6 weeks over schedule due to scope expansion requested by the client midway through.
Contract amendment was processed but late, creating a cash flow gap in Q3 2025.
Lessons learned: scope gates need to be built into the SOW more explicitly for
transformation engagements in the automotive sector. Client satisfaction: 3.7.
Relationship is intact but the account manager has flagged renewal risk for the
follow-on implementation phase.

EMPIRE RETAIL GROUP — Supply Chain Optimization (P2303)
At-risk project that ended with a partial deliverable. Client experienced an internal
reorganization that changed the executive sponsor three times during our engagement.
Invoiced $320K of a $500K contract. Final satisfaction score not collected.
Decision was made to not pursue further work with this client given the organizational
instability. Engagement Manager: Robert Mitchell.

PRACTICE-SPECIFIC OBSERVATIONS — Q1 2026
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Technology & Digital:
The practice is over-capacity. Seven active projects as of April 30, with an eighth
scheduled to kick off in June. Three senior consultants are running at 95%+ billable
utilization, which is unsustainable beyond Q2. The practice lead has escalated a
request for two additional hires at the Senior Consultant level to the Partners.
Pipeline weighted value in this practice is the highest in the firm at $6.2M.

Strategy & Growth:
The M&A advisory pipeline is strong but lumpy. Three large opportunities (>$500K each)
are expected to close in Q2/Q3. Loss of one Partner in February 2026 has created a
coverage gap in the Northeast region that is being backfilled by a Principal on a
temporary basis.

HR & People:
The practice had its slowest quarter in two years in Q4 2025, primarily due to budget
freezes at two major clients (financial services sector). Q1 2026 shows some recovery
with two new project starts. The practice leader is exploring a workforce analytics
sub-offering that could differentiate MCG in the HRtech advisory space.

Finance & Risk:
Steady performance. Regulatory compliance mandates (particularly in the banking sector)
are generating inbound interest without heavy sales effort. The practice is close to
full capacity, with utilization averaging 80% in Q1 2026.

Operations Excellence:
A few older client relationships in manufacturing are at natural end-of-scope and have
not renewed. The practice is actively prospecting in the energy sector, where operational
efficiency mandates are being driven by ESG reporting requirements.
